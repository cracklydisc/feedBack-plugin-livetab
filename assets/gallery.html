Family 1 — segmented

Pick one of a small fixed set. An inset track of cells that light up.

four is the most a row holds

Past four it wraps, and the count decides — not the author. Five named labels in one 360px row get about 66px each, which truncates a word like Sight-reading. So they break onto a second line and stop pretending to be a joined switch: each takes its own ground and its own corner, sized to its label rather than to a share, and the container drops its well — a box round two ragged lines only says the box is the wrong shape.

five options · wrapped · amber mark = changed underneath

The mark is the fact a label cannot carry. “This is the preset you picked” is the chip being lit; “and you have changed something under it” is a second fact about the same chip, so it cannot be another chip.

Family 1b — select

The third answer to “pick one”, and the same rule reaches it: the option count decides. Up to four a segmented row, past that wrapping chips, and past the point where chips would be a wall — or when the app supplies the list at runtime so nobody knows how many there will be — the choice collapses to the one that is current.

It is a native <select>. A hand-drawn sheet was built here first and withdrawn: what it cost was keyboard, type-ahead, screen readers, a gamepad and a list allowed to be taller than the panel. What stays ours is the well it sits in — the chassis, the corner, the chevron in its own lit square, inert so the press reaches the element underneath.

The rack — chassis, racks, wells

The panel is a chassis and each block is a rack bolted into it: a label, a read-only aside in the label's row, a header slot for one control set once, and a body. Racks are flat and divided by a 1px stroke rather than by air — which is why the space scale got denser when this arrived. A well is the opposite gesture: a slot cut into the chassis, darker and rounder, meaning "readout, not control".

Status, list rows, and the folded strip

A status line's chrome scales with urgency: a message that is merely true gets a line, one that has to stop you gets cut into the chassis and outlined, and it carries its own way out — because a blocked state you cannot act on is a dead end. And "ok" is silent by default: everything is normal is a signal that carries nothing.

A list row is h-md, because it is pressed while the song is running. Its meter is an LED bar — segments rather than a smooth fill, because what it reports is discrete, and at 8% a smooth meter is a sliver you cannot see while one lit cell in twelve is unambiguous.

The folded strip is the panel at reading size, and it holds no buttons: the whole block is the target, because the one thing you might want mid-song is "give me the rest of it" and aiming at a chevron with a guitar in your hands is not a gesture.
